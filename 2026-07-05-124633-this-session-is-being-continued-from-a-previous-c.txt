  What's wrong: the kernel re-dequantizes the whole weight every forward pass

  Our nvfp4_w4a16_matmul_cuda, on every call (every Linear, every one of the 4
  denoise steps, every image), does:

  1. enqueue_create_buffer[bf16](N*K) — allocate a full dense bf16 weight
  2. enqueue_fp4_materialize — dequantize the entire 4-bit weight → bf16, 
  writing 2·N·K bytes to VRAM
  3. _matmul_gpu — then the ordinary bf16 GEMM reads that 2·N·K back

  So per Linear we move ~4.5·N·K bytes of weight traffic (read fp4 + write bf16
  + read bf16) versus bf16's 2·N·K (read resident weight once) — ~2.25× the 
  weight DRAM traffic, plus double the kernel launches (a materialize and a GEMM
  instead of one GEMM). At 128², M (tokens) is tiny, the GEMM is trivial, and
  this fixed dequant cost dominates. Measured: 0.66 s/img NVFP4 vs your ~0.17 
  s/img bf16 ≈ 4×. The 4× (vs the ~2.25× traffic) is the extra kernel-launch
  overhead piling up across hundreds of Linears × 4 steps × 6 images at tiny M.

  It is not a wiring bug — it's exactly what materialize→dense is designed to
  do. We chose it for correctness + the VRAM win (weights stay 4-bit resident),
  explicitly not for speed. The cost is proportional to weight size, independent
  of image size — so it's worst at 128² and amortizes as images grow (at 1024²
  the GEMM dominates and NVFP4 ≈ bf16).

  The real fix (this is what Phase D is for)

  A fused mixed-input GEMM — dequantize each 4-bit weight tile inline in the
  GEMM mainloop (in registers/shared memory), or use sm_120's native FP4 tensor
  cores. Then we read only 0.5·N·K (the 4-bit weight) and never materialize bf16
  — that's 4× less weight bandwidth than bf16. In the small-M (bandwidth-bound)
  regime, NVFP4 would become faster than bf16, exactly as quantization should
  be — while keeping the 4-bit VRAM win. That's a ~8–16× swing from where we are
  at 128².

  The key reframe: the slowdown you saw is the worst case. Nobody serves 128² in
  production; at real resolutions the gap largely closes on its own.
